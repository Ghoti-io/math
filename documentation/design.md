# Design

**Status:** `sin`, `cos`, `tan`, `atan2`, `exp`, `log`, `pow` and `hypot` are implemented (CAP-1), with the exact functions of `basic.h` and the decimal conversions of `decimal.h`.

Deterministic elementary functions that give the same bits on every host.

The dependency rules and the determinism gates that bind this library are in
the Makefile's header comment and in `tools/`: `check-fp-contract`,
`check-edges`, `check-libm` and `check-no-x87`, each shown to fail on a planted
violation by `make check-gates`.

## sin, cos and tan

Written from published algorithms and the oracle's results only; no library's
source was read.

**Reduction.** For `|x| > pi/4`, `x * 2/pi` is formed exactly enough by the
Payne-Hanek method (Payne and Hanek, 1983): the integer-valued bits of `2/pi`
beyond the window that can matter are multiples of 4 and dropped; a 256-bit
window of the bits below is multiplied by `x`'s 53-bit significand in 32-bit
limbs with 64-bit products. That gives the quadrant (`N mod 4`, with the
fraction rounded to the nearest integer) and a 256-bit fraction in `[-1/2, 1/2]`,
whose leading 106 bits times `pi/2` (a double-double) give `r = rh + rl`,
`|r| <= pi/4`, to a relative error of about `2^-100`. The smallest fraction any
double has is about `2^-61` (`6381956970095103 * 2^797`), so the window's
truncation error of `2^-168` is far below one ulp of the result. Only integer
arithmetic is used: a 128-bit type is not available on i686 and an integer to
double conversion compiles to an x87 `fild` there, which `check-no-x87` refuses,
so a 53-bit integer becomes a double through the bit pattern of `2^52 + k`.
The table is indexed from the exponent of `x`, so `|x|` up to `DBL_MAX` takes
the same code as `|x| = 1`.

**Fast reduction.** For `pi/4 < |x| < 2^20` the quotient is one multiplication
(`n = round(x * 2/pi)`, taken through the `2^52 + 2^51` trick, which needs no
integer to double conversion) and the remainder is `x - n*P1 - n*P2 - n*P3 -
n*P4` with `pi/2 = P1 + P2 + P3 + P4`, `P1..P3` of 33 bits and `P4` a double
(Cody and Waite, 1980). Every product with `P1..P3` is exact because `n` has at
most 20 bits, `x - n*P1` is exact, and the sums are Knuth's TwoSum, so the
remainder is a double-double accurate to `2^-134` absolute. That is
`2^-79` of the remainder or better for `|r| >= 2^-55`, and the fast path hands
anything smaller (no double below `2^20` is closer than `2^-60` to a multiple
of `pi/2`) to Payne-Hanek, as it does a quotient the rounding put one off
(`|r|` above the double `pi/4`). Where it answers it returns the same `n` and
the same `rh` as Payne-Hanek and an `rl` that differs by under `2^-79 |r|`
(measured at most `2^-87`), so the results are the same bits: the committed
hash over the original 46,569 results did not change. `trig.c` states the
proof, `test_trig_reduce.cpp` compares the two reductions, and `make bench`
times them.

**Wide fast reduction.** For `2^20 <= |x| < 2^30` the same method with narrower
parts, because `n` now has up to 30 bits: `pi/2 = Q1 + ... + Q5 + Q6` with
`Q1..Q5` of 23 bits (so `n*Qk` is exact) and `Q6` a double. `x - n*Q1` and the
next subtraction are exact (the second because its result has at most 48 bits),
the following three sums are TwoSum, and the remainder is accurate to
`2^-104 |r| + 2^-141` absolute, which is `2^-84 |r|` at the same `2^-55` floor.
It returns the same `n`, `rh` and (to `2^-79 |r|`) `rl` as Payne-Hanek, so the
hash over every result that existed before it did not change. Arguments from
`2^30` up, and any it declines, take Payne-Hanek (about 53 ns on the build host
against about 10 ns for the fast paths).

**Kernel.** A table-driven kernel (Tang, 1991; Muller, Elementary Functions,
chapters 4 and 5) for the reduced argument `h + l`, `0 <= h <= pi/4`. Write
`h = a + b` with `a` the leading six bits of `h`'s mantissa (64 slices per
binade for `2^-4 <= h < 1`, so 256 values of `a` and `0 <= b < a/64`). Row `k`
of a 257-row, 64-byte-row table (16 KB, `src/elementary/trig_kernel_table.h`)
holds `sin a` and `cos a` as double-doubles, `tan a` and `cot a` as doubles,
and the exact residuals `d0 = sin_hi - tan_hi cos_hi` and
`e0 = cos_hi - cot_hi sin_hi`; the last row is `a = 0`, the direct path below
`2^-4`. Only the correction `sin(a + b + l) - sin a` is computed in double,
from `ups ~ sin b - b` and `cosm1 ~ cos b - 1` (degree 3 minimax polynomials in
`b^2`) and the table: it is under 1.7% of the result, so its roundings cost a
few hundredths of an ulp and the table value and the final addition carry the
precision. `cos` is the same formula with the table's columns exchanged, and
`tan` is `tan a + ((cs - tan a cc) + d0) / (cos_hi + cc)` from the two
corrections, one plain division of a quantity below 3% of the result (odd
quadrants: `cot`, with `e0`). Below `2^-4`, `tan` is a degree 6 polynomial, and
the cotangent of a small remainder a refined reciprocal of a double-double.
Everything is generated by `tools/oracle/fit_kernel.py` at 220 bits and fitted by
Lawson's weighted least squares (a published minimax method).

This replaced a double-double kernel (`r^3/6`, `r^2/2` and `r^4/24` in
double-double, and for `tan` a double-double quotient of the two kernels),
which was correct to 0.50x ulp and cost 24 ns for `sin`, 21 for `cos` and 57
for `tan` at `|x| <= pi/4` (`make bench`, Core 7 150U, GCC 16.2, one core
pinned, loop and call overhead of about 3 ns included). The new kernel takes 8,
9 and 11 ns there, 2.5 to 5 times faster, and 1.6 to 2.7 times faster up to
`2^20`, where the reduction is most of the time. The price is that results are
no longer the nearest double so often: 0.1 to 0.5% differ from the old
kernel's, always by one ulp, and the worst measured error is 0.52 ulp for `sin`
and `cos` and 0.57 for `tan` (`tests/unit/test_trig.cpp`). The accuracy budget is
written in `trig.c`; it is measured, not proved. A vector kernel can be added
later without changing any result: the table row is one cache line and the
formulas are written lane by lane (`kernel_front`, `kernel_tails`,
`kernel_correction`).

**Special values.** `|x| < 2^-28` returns `x` for `sin` and `tan`, and `|x| <
2^-27` returns 1 for `cos`, which is within the contract and is exact for
subnormals and zeros. An infinite or NaN argument gives the quiet NaN
`0x7ff8000000000000` on every target (the sign and payload of the NaN an IEEE
operation makes differs between x86 and the others, so it is fixed), and an
infinity raises invalid.

**Checks.** See the README. The unit test holds the committed corpus to 0.515,
0.521 and 0.535 ulp (sin, cos, tan) as well as to the contract of 1, so that a
wrong table entry is seen; the committed hash catches what even that cannot (one
ulp of a polynomial's low coefficients), `test_trig_kernel.cpp` checks every row
of the table against an independent series, and `check-planted.py` says which
perturbations no test can see and why.


## exp, log and pow

Written from published algorithms and the oracle's results only (Tang, 1989
and 1990; Muller, Elementary Functions, chapters 4 to 7); no library's source
was read. `src/elementary/explog.c`, with the tables and polynomials generated
by `tools/oracle/fit_explog.py` (`explog_tables.h`).

**exp.** `x = n ln2/128 + r`, `|r| <= ln2/256`, and `exp x = 2^(n div 128)
2^((n mod 128)/128) exp r`. `n` is the nearest integer to `x 128/ln2` through the
`2^52 + 2^51` trick (no integer to double conversion). `ln2/128` is split in two
parts, the first of 30 bits so that `n L1` is exact, and `x - n L1` is exact too, so
`r` costs one rounding (`2^-61.5`). `2^(j/128)` is a table of 128 double-doubles
(2 KB) and `exp r - 1 = r + r^2 P(r)` has a degree 3 polynomial fitted to `2^-65`;
`exp x = T_hi + (T_lo + T_hi (r + r^2 P))`, so the only rounding of the size of the
result is the last addition. The scale `2^k` is a bit pattern, exact; above 708
or below -708 it is applied in two steps (and a result in the subnormal range is
rounded twice, within 0.75 ulp). The thresholds (710 and -746) decide infinity
and zero; a result that crosses the overflow or the underflow boundary in between
does so by the multiplication, which raises the exception. `1 + x` is returned below
`2^-54`.

**log.** `x = 2^e m`, `m` in `[0.707, 1.414)` (a bit-pattern offset instead of a
test), cut into 128 rows by the top seven bits of the offset pattern. Row `j`
holds a multiplier `c_j` with 26 significant bits near `1/m`, `t_j = -log c_j` as a
high part that is a multiple of `2^-41` and a low part. Then `z = c m - 1` is
formed exactly in two parts (the high 27 bits of `m` times `c` and the rest
times `c` are exact products, and a FastTwoSum gives `zh + zl`), and `log x =
e ln2_hi + t_hi + [z + z^2 P(z)] + (e ln2_lo + t_lo + zl)`, `P` a degree 5 polynomial
accurate to `2^-60.4` of `z`. The two rows that touch 1.0 have `c = 1` and `t = 0`, so
for `x` near 1 the result is `log1p` of the exact `z = x - 1` and nothing cancels; for
every other row the smallest `|t_hi|` exceeds the largest `|z|`, which `fit_explog.py`
asserts and which is what makes the FastTwoSum of `A` and `z` exact. Measured
0.5048 ulp over the corpus.

**pow.** `exp(y log x)` with the logarithm as a double-double (`log_dd`:
the same rows, `z^2` as the exact sum of two products, a cubic term in double
accurate to `2^-69.5` of `z`, and the `zl (1 - zh)` of the reduced argument's low part; relative error `2^-67`), the product `y log x` as a
double-double (Dekker's product, the halves cut by masking), and `exp_core` taking
`n` from `wh + wl` and `r` from both parts. The error of the logarithm is multiplied
by `|y log x| <= 746`, so the worst results (`x` near 1, `y` huge, a result near
the overflow) are within about 0.51 ulp (measured); an exactly representable result is exact.
The special values follow C99 F.9.4.4, in the order the standard states them, and
are all in the corpus (the specials crossed with the specials) and in
`test_elementary.cpp` row by row. A finite negative `x` uses `|x|` and the parity
of `y`, from the bits of `y` (`integer_class`), so `y` up to and past `2^53` is
right. A `y` of `2^64` or more is decided by the sign of `log x` alone, which
avoids the overflow of the product.

## atan2

`src/elementary/atan2.c`, with the table generated by `tools/oracle/fit_atan2.py`
(`atan2_table.h`). With `u = min(|y|, |x|)`, `v = max`, `q = u/v`, the result is
`B + s atan q`, `(B, s)` being `(0, +)`, `(pi/2, -)`, `(pi, -)` or `(pi/2, +)` by the
signs and the swap, `B` a double-double and the sum a FastTwoSum (the octant
reflections are exact, not a source of error). For `q` in `[1/8, 1]`, `F` is the
nearest multiple of 1/64 and `atan q = atan F + G1 beta + ... + G8 beta^8`, the Taylor
series at `F` (a row of ten doubles, 128 rows so that the masked index is inside the
table), `beta = (u - F v)/v` with `u - F v` formed exactly in two parts. Below `1/8` there
is no table: `q` is a double-double `qh + ql` (the rounded quotient `u (1/v)`, cut to 26
bits, and the exact remainder `u - qh v` times `1/v`; one division in all) and `atan q = q + q^3
P(q^2)`, `P` of degree 6. Below `2^-30` the quotient `u/v` is the result (`q^2/3`
is `2^-61`), and handles every exponent; a `v` outside `[2^-600, 2^600]` is scaled.
Measured 0.5345 ulp over the corpus.

## hypot

`src/elementary/hypot.c`. With `a >= b` scaled by a power of two when `a` is outside `[2^-300,
2^300]`, `a^2 + b^2` is formed as an exact sum of products (`a = ah + al` with `ah` of 26 bits),
`s0` is `gmath_sqrt` of its rounded value (correctly rounded, so `sqrt(S)` to `2^-53`), `c` is `s0`
with 26 bits so that `c^2` is exact, and `h = c + (S - c^2)/(c + s0)`, which is `sqrt` to better than
`2^-60`. The result is within 0.5 ulp. If `b < 2^-27 a`, `a` is the result. An infinity beats a
NaN, as C99 F.9.4.3 says. There is no spurious overflow (the scale) or underflow. Story 4 computed
the square root here from a table and two Newton steps; story 4a replaced that by the library's own
`gmath_sqrt` (below), and `hypot` is faster by about a quarter on a target with the instruction (4.6 ns against 6.3, 6.0 against 8.3, 4.5 against 6.2).

## sqrt, the rounding functions and the exponent functions (story 4a)

AD-22 as amended on 2026-10-18 has `math` supply everything the shape family takes from `<math.h>`,
and `make check-libm` refuses every libm symbol in `math` itself and in the three shape libraries.
`src/elementary/sqrt_impl.h`, `sqrt.c`, `round.c`, `scale.c`; public in `basic.h`.

`gmath_sqrt` is correctly rounded (IEEE 754 requires it of `sqrt`), so every implementation gives
the same bits. Two are provided and gated against each other:

* **the instruction** (`sqrtsd` on x86-64 and on i686 with SSE math, `fsqrt` on aarch64, `sqdbr` on
  s390x, `fsqrt` on powerpc when the compiler says the optional instruction exists
  (`_ARCH_PPCSQ`), `fsqrtd` on sparc64, `fsqrt.d` on riscv with a double-precision FPU), through
  inline assembly. Not `__builtin_sqrt`: that is a call to libm unless the compiler is told
  `-fno-math-errno`, and often at `-O0` regardless, and a call is what the link gate refuses. The
  library builds with `-fno-math-errno` as well. The instruction's NaN has a sign and payload of its
  own choosing (x86 makes it negative), so a NaN result is replaced by the library's one quiet NaN.
* **the portable method**, in integer and double arithmetic only: the argument is scaled to `m` in
  `[1, 4)` by an even power of two (a subnormal first by `2^54`, exactly), `1/sqrt(m)` comes from the
  512-entry start that `hypot` used to own (`hypot_table.h`, within `2^-10`) and two Newton steps
  (`2^-38`), `c` is `m r` with 26 bits so that `c^2` and `R = m - c^2` are exact, and
  `y = c + R (r/2)(1 + (s0 - c) r/2)`, which is the root to within `0.5 ulp + 2^-61 y`. The
  correctly rounded root is then `y` or a neighbour, and an exact test of the residual
  `rho = m - y^2` (formed as `(m - p) - e` with `p + e = y^2` by Dekker's product, `y` split in two
  halves of 26 bits by rounding) chooses: `y` rounds up iff `rho > y u` and down iff `rho <= -y u`
  with `u = 2^-52`. No ties exist (the square of a midpoint has 107 bits and `m` has 53), and the `u^2/4`
  that the exact boundaries carry is below the granularity `2^-104` of `rho`, which is why one is a
  strict and the other a non-strict comparison. `testSqrt_internal` hands the correction an estimate
  that is one ulp wrong each way, since the estimate in practice only falls short and the round-down
  branch and the equality cases would otherwise never run.

`-DGMATH_SQRT_PORTABLE` builds the whole library, `hypot` included, on the portable method.
`gmath_sqrt_portable` and `gmath_sqrt_hardware` are forced entry points; `gmath_sqrt_has_hardware`
says whether the instruction exists. `corpus-hash` runs both over the corpus and exits 1 when they
differ, and `suite/tools/xarch/shape.sh` builds the probe with and without the flag on every target.

`floor`, `ceil`, `trunc`, `round` (half away from zero), `rint` (half to even; the rounding mode is
not read), `fabs`, `copysign`: on the bit pattern. With `e` the unbiased exponent and `0 <= e <= 51`
the low `52 - e` stored bits are the fraction, masked off or added to by integer operations; a
`floor` or `ceil` that must move by one adds `+-1.0` to an integer of magnitude below `2^52`, which is
exact. Zero results keep the sign Annex F gives them. `scalbn` (= `ldexp`) writes the exponent field,
and for a subnormal result shifts the integer significand with one round to nearest even, so there is
no double rounding; `frexp` and `ilogb` read it. Each has one correct answer, so the oracle for them
is the exact reference (`tools/oracle/gen_corpus_exact.py`: MPFR and exact rationals or integers,
which must agree), and the test requires equality to the bit. Not provided, because nothing needs
them and the gate refuses them: `fmod`, `modf`, `fma` (AD-17 forbids fusing), `lrint` and its kin,
`fmin`, `fmax`, `cbrt`, `exp2`, `log2` and the float (`f`) forms.

## Decimal conversion

`gmath_decimal_parse`, `gmath_decimal_to_double` and `gmath_decimal_shortest` (`decimal.h`;
`src/decimal/`). A decimal is rounded to the nearest `double`, ties to even, and a `double` is
written as the shortest decimal that rounds back to it. Both are exact, so the oracle is exact
too, and the test requires equality. Both use integer arithmetic only, in a fixed-size integer
of 128 32-bit limbs (`bignum.h`). So nothing depends on the target, the compiler, the locale or
the rounding mode, nothing allocates, and no floating-point exception is raised. IEEE 754's
inexact, overflow and underflow (tininess before rounding) come back as flags. Written from the
definitions below and the oracle's results; no implementation's source was read. The method is
in the spirit of Clinger's (1990) exact fallback and of Steele and White's (1990) free-format
output: exact integer arithmetic, with no approximation to correct afterwards.

**Reading.** The value is `D * 10^E` with `D` an integer, its leading and trailing zeros removed.

* *Long inputs.* Every double, and every midpoint between two consecutive doubles, has at most
  768 significant decimal digits. A midpoint is `(2M + 1) * 2^q` with `M < 2^53` and
  `q >= -1075`. For `q < 0` that is `(2M + 1) * 5^-q / 10^-q`, and `(2M + 1) * 5^1075` has at
  most `ceil(54 log10 2 + 1075 log10 5) = 768` digits. For `q >= 0` it is an integer below
  `2^1025`. So when more than 800 significant digits are given, the value lies strictly between
  its first 800 digits `P` and the next 800-digit decimal `P + 10^e`. No rounding boundary lies
  in that open interval: a boundary near the value starts at most one decimal place above the
  value's leading digit and ends at most 767 places below that, so it is a multiple of `10^e`.
  Every point in the interval therefore rounds alike, and `P` followed by the digit 5 stands for
  the value. An input of any length costs one pass over its digits and the work of 801 digits.
* *Range.* A leading digit at `10^309` or above is infinity, since the largest double plus half
  a unit is below `1.8 * 10^308`. One at `10^-325` or below is zero, since `10^-324` is below
  `2^-1075`, half the smallest subnormal. Both are decided from the exponent alone, by
  saturating arithmetic. The caller's exponent may be any `int64_t`, and the parser saturates
  its exponent at `2^61`, which no input shorter than `2^62` bytes can bring back into range.
* *The integer.* Otherwise `E` is in `[-1125, 308]`, and the value is `A / B` with
  `A = D * 10^max(E, 0)` and `B = 10^max(-E, 0)`. `B` has at most 3,738 bits. With `la` and `lb`
  the bit lengths, `k = 56 - la + lb` makes `q = floor(A * 2^k / B)` lie in `(2^55, 2^57)`.
  When `B` is 1, `q` is the top 57 bits of `A`. The dividend has at most `lb + 57` bits, so
  4,096 bits hold every operand. `q` and a flag for a nonzero remainder are rounded once: to 53
  bits when the leading bit's exponent `ue` is at least -1022, and to `ue + 1075` bits below
  that, so a subnormal is rounded once at its own precision. The 2 to 4 bits below the kept
  ones and the flag decide the rounding, ties to even. The rounded significand of a subnormal is
  its own encoding, so a carry into `2^52` is the smallest normal number with no special case.
  A carry out of a normal significand moves the exponent, and may overflow.
* *Divisions, three ways, all exact.* No division when `B` is 1. Short division by one 32-bit
  limb when `B * 2^max(-k, 0)` fits one, which covers a fraction of up to nine digits next to a
  short integer, the usual shape of SVG and CSS. Restoring binary long division otherwise, which
  is the general tier. The tests run every corpus input through both its own tier and the
  general one, require the same bits and flags, and require each tier to be taken more than a
  hundred times (AD-13).

**Writing.** With `x = m * 2^e`, the decimals that round to `x` form the interval between the
midpoints next to `x`. Its ends are included when `m` is even (a tie goes to the even
neighbour, which is `x`) and excluded when `m` is odd. The interval is `x +- 2^(e-1)`, except
that below a power of two other than the smallest normal number the lower end is
`x - 2^(e-2)`, since the predecessor is in the binade below. So `x`, the upper end and the lower
end are `4m`, `4m + 2` and `4m - 2` (or `4m - 1`) times `2^(e-2)`.

* *One division each.* `L` is the power of ten of `x`'s leading digit, estimated from the
  binary exponent and confirmed by the quotient that follows. With `s = L - 16`, each of the
  three values is divided once, exactly, by `10^s`. That gives an integer of 17 digits and a
  flag for a nonzero remainder; for `x`, the remainder is also compared with one half.
* *The search.* For `n = 1, ..., 17`, the candidates with `n` significant digits are the
  integers `c` with `c * 10^(17-n)` inside the scaled interval. The bounds, and the candidate
  nearest `x` (half to even), are integer divisions of the three 17-digit integers by
  `10^(17-n)`, with the flags deciding the exact cases. The first `n` with a candidate is the
  shortest length. The nearest candidate inside the bounds is the answer, which clamping gives,
  since all the candidates lie on one side of a nearest integer that falls outside.
* *Seventeen digits suffice.* At `n = 17` the scaled interval is wider than one unit, so it
  holds an integer. Its width is `2^(e-1) * 2 / 10^s`, at least `2^-53 * x / 10^s > 2^-53 * 10^16 > 1.1`.
  Below a power of two it is three quarters of the unit in the last place, and there
  `ulp / x = 2^-52`, so the width is at least `0.75 * 2^-52 * 10^16 > 1.6`.
* *Two fast tiers*, each checked against the general tier on every corpus input and shown
  taken:
  * An integer below `2^53` is its own shortest spelling. Its interval is at most one unit
    wide, so any other decimal in it is at least as long, and a decimal with fewer significant
    digits, being a multiple of a higher power of ten, is at least one unit away.
  * When `s <= 0` and `e < 2` (every `|x|` below `10^16` that is not an integer), the divisor
    is a power of two and the three divisions are shifts.

**Gates.**
* *The corpus.* `tests/corpus/decimal_parse.corpus` and `decimal_shortest.corpus` are written by
  `tools/oracle/gen_corpus_decimal.py`, with three references that must agree. For reading:
  the exact rational value rounded by Python's correctly rounded integer division, MPFR, and
  CPython's `float()`. For writing: an exact interval search over rationals written there, and
  CPython's `repr()`.
* *Corpus inputs.* Midpoints, written exactly and as 801 to 900 digits just above and below
  them. Ties carried by a short and by a long fraction. The thresholds of overflow and
  underflow. Every power of two, and the neighbours of every power of ten. Integers past `2^54`,
  whose interval ends are even integers.
* *Cross-target hash.* Both functions are in the corpus hash, outside its total, so the
  cross-target scripts compare them.
* *Unit tests.* `testDecimal` checks the grammar's edges, the arguments refused, inputs of
  100,000 digits, the inverse property over 200,000 random doubles, and the same results in
  every rounding mode with no exception raised.
* *Planted defects.* `make check-planted-decimal` plants 40 defects, in the truncation, the
  early decisions, the rounding, the grammar, the search and the integer operations, and requires
  each to fail. Its header says what is deliberately not planted, and why.

Not provided: hexadecimal floating point, the spelling of infinity and NaN, digit separators, and
any formatting of the digits (`1e23` against `1.0E+23` is the caller's). A format stricter than
the shared grammar (JSON forbids `+1`, `.5` and `01`) checks its own rule.

## Exact binary remainder

`gmath_fmod` and `gmath_fmodf` use integer arithmetic on the IEEE significand
and exponent. For positive normalized operands `x = mx * 2^(ex-p)` and
`y = my * 2^(ey-p)`, with `p` fraction bits, the remainder in units of
`2^(ey-p)` is `(mx * 2^(ex-ey)) mod my`. Reducing before each bounded shift
keeps every intermediate in 64 bits: chunks are 11 bits for binary64 and 40
bits for binary32. The normalized initial significands differ by less than a
factor of two, so the initial reduction needs only a comparison/subtraction.
Small exponent gaps need one division. Power-of-two divisors use a mask, and
two subnormal inputs can be reduced directly in their common integer unit.

The nonzero result fits in the input format exactly: it is smaller than the
divisor and a multiple of the smaller input's binary unit. The final packing
therefore discards only zero bits. No floating-point arithmetic is needed on
the valid path, so rounding modes and underflow/inexact flags do not affect it.
Leading-bit counts use a compiler intrinsic where available, with an integer
loop fallback tested by `GMATH_REMAINDER_PORTABLE_BITS`. Neither function sets
`errno`. NaNs use the existing canonical policy; signaling NaNs in either
operand raise invalid, even when the other operand is a quiet NaN.

`tools/oracle/gen_corpus_remainder.py` independently computes the quotient and
remainder as unbounded rational numbers, then asserts exact representability.
It generates normal exponent sweeps, subnormal bit lengths, extreme exponent
gaps, exact multiples and their neighbors, special values, and seeded random
pairs. Binary32 corpus words are stored as exactly widened binary64 values;
raw float signaling-NaN and exception cases are tested separately. The corpus
hash probe reports both new functions outside the legacy aggregate.
The local `check-corpus-hash` gate checks those supplemental hashes. The suite's
existing cross-target scripts compare the legacy aggregate, so they do not yet
gate remainder results across architectures. Their integration is deferred
while other repositories have independent work in progress; no cross-target
remainder run is claimed here.

The remainder benchmark compares both formats over ordinary quotients, nearby
magnitudes, large exponent gaps, subnormal inputs, power-of-two divisors, and
dividends smaller than divisors. On the Core 7 150U host with GCC 14.2, glibc
2.41, `-O2`, one CPU pinned and best of 200 repeats, the initial measurement
puts ordinary double remainder at about 3 ns versus 2.3 ns for libm. Float
remainder with a 200-bit exponent gap takes about 10 ns versus 55 ns. The
1800-bit double gap remains slower, about 546 ns versus 341 ns. Performance
is input-dependent and broad speed superiority remains future work.

## Exact minimum and maximum

`gmath_fmin`, `gmath_fmax` and their float forms derive unsigned ordering keys
from each IEEE encoding: complement all active bits for a negative number,
or flip the sign bit for a positive one. The resulting keys order numbers
numerically and put -0 below +0. Masks select the original encoding, so normal
and subnormal results are exact without floating arithmetic or exceptions.
The first sign-dependent conditional version made GCC generate unpredictable
branches; explicit masks removed those branches from the numeric path.

The NaN policy follows the documented GNU libc fmin/fmax behavior:
https://sourceware.org/glibc/manual/2.41/html_node/Misc-FP-Arithmetic.html.
A single quiet NaN yields the other input; two quiet NaNs yield the library's
canonical NaN. Either signaling NaN yields that NaN and raises invalid. Signed
zero preference is fixed in both operand orders, a deterministic refinement
where host libm choices can differ. No function changes errno or rounding mode.

The independent corpus oracle compares finite values as rationals and handles
IEEE special values explicitly, with fixed-answer self-checks. Raw float NaNs
are tested separately so widening does not hide their signaling status.
Supplemental hashes leave all older results and the legacy aggregate unchanged.
As with remainder, suite cross-target enforcement of the supplemental hashes
is pending; local host checks are active.

On the same Core 7 150U host, GCC 14.2, glibc 2.41, `-O2`, one CPU pinned and
best of 200 repeats, all four numeric extrema calls measure about 2 ns including
indirect call/loop overhead, approximately matching host libm. These are scalar
call measurements, not application throughput or compiler-inlined comparisons.

## Binary and decimal logarithms

`gmath_log2` and `gmath_log10` normalize the existing `log_dd` result without
rounding its sum, multiply the high part by the high reciprocal with Dekker's
exact residual, retain all low/cross terms, and round at the last addition.
`tools/oracle/fit_logbase.py` generates 1/ln(2) and 1/ln(10) as double-double
constants at 240 bits and checks them independently with MPFR to better than
2^-105 relative. Existing log, exp and pow expression ordering and constants
are unchanged.

The shared logarithm arithmetic body is factored into `log_dd_parts`, with
the original natural-log wrapper retaining its original normalization sequence.
New base-log callers normalize subnormals by integer bits, producing exactly
the same bits as multiplying by 2^52 and an exponent adjustment of -52. This
also avoids a Clang optimization that speculated that floating multiplication
on huge normal inputs and raised a spurious overflow. GCC and Clang are both
checked for value and exception behavior.

The precision reserve comes from the documented approximately 2^-67 relative
accuracy of `log_dd`; after normalization the low-product roundoffs are about
2^-106 of the result. This gives substantial margin beyond the final half-ulp
rounding, while the public target remains one ulp and requires round-to-nearest.
Positive binary powers are detected before logarithm arithmetic and return
their exact exponent, including all subnormal powers, without inexact.

The first float version reused the full double kernel and was over five times
host log2f on common inputs. The final path uses the same table but takes
advantage of binary32's 24 significant bits: the product of m and the table's
26-bit multiplier fits exactly in double, and subtraction of 1 is exact. A
five-term log1p Taylor series has remainder bounded by
`|z|^6 / (6 * (1 - |z|))` for `|z|` below about 0.008. The rows touching 1 have
zero table logarithm, preserving relative accuracy near the singular zero
result. Other rows are separated from 1. The integer exponent is added in base
two, then the result is narrowed once to binary32. Correct rounding of every
float result is not a promise; the one-ulp target has substantial reserve.

A conservative combined binary32 budget uses `u = 2^-53`, `|z| < 0.008`,
`|table_log| < 0.36` and integer exponents of magnitude below 150. Product and
subtraction forming z are exact. Away from the two rows touching one, the
result's magnitude is at least 2^-8, so its float ulp is at least 2^-31.
The Taylor remainder times 1/ln(2), plus the deliberately generous roundoff
allowance `64*u*(150 + 0.36 + 0.008)`, is below 0.003 of that smallest ulp.
For the rows touching one with exponent zero, there is no table cancellation:
`|log(1+z)| >= |z|/(1+|z|)`, giving under 0.0001 float ulp from truncation and
roundoff. Nonzero exponents in those rows have results bounded away from zero.
Thus a conservative pre-round allowance of 0.01 ulp leaves room for the final
nearest float rounding within the 0.51 regression target. An independent
double-double atanh-series test checks every table row before float narrowing;
its measured pre-round maximum is about 0.000059 float ulp.

The MPFR600/mpmath640 oracle rounds rationals directly to the target precision
and stores signed error in target-format ulps. It tests binary powers and
neighbors, all table boundaries, values near 1, decimal powers, subnormals and
seeded random values. A separate seed with over 322,000 reference inputs across

0.51-ulp regression margin in addition to the public one-ulp contract.

## Binary exponential

`gmath_exp2` reduces directly in base two: `n = round(128*x)`,
`d = x - n/128`, `k = floor(n/128)` and `j = n mod 128`. Both power-of-two
scalings and the subtraction are exact in the active range; for tiny values
whose n is zero, subtraction of zero is exact. The small residual d is
converted by split ln(2), then passed to the existing exp polynomial/table.
The reconstruction arithmetic is factored into `exp_reconstruct` without
changing the original exp/pow expression order or any existing result hash.

The exact binary reduction avoids amplifying a rounded ln(2) error over a
range spanning more than two thousand exponents. Its converted residual
stays within the existing fitted polynomial interval. The final table addition
dominates the normal rounding error; subnormal scaling adds a second rounding,
with an approximately 0.75-ulp budget. The public contract remains one ulp.
The tests use 0.52 normal and 0.76 subnormal regression margins.

Integer inputs are recognized from j=0 and d=0 and return packed exponent bits,
including subnormal powers. Tiny `|x|<=2^-54` returns `1+x`, which rounds to 1,
avoiding tiny residual products and spurious underflow. A magnitude test keeps
ordinary signed inputs away from the special-value ladder. Finite x>=1024
overflows; finite x<=-1075 rounds to zero. Input spacing near the upper limit
ensures every representable x<1024 has a finite result, even when k=1024.

Normal scaling is exact exponent-bit adjustment. For a subnormal result the
intermediate is first scaled exactly into the normal range, then multiplied
by 2^-1000. A guarded volatile operand keeps that tiny product on its actual
branch under optimizers that do not observe floating exceptions. Noninteger
binary-rational x has an inexact 2^x, so underflow/inexact is raised explicitly
even if the rounded intermediate happens to scale exactly. Integer powers
return before this branch and retain their no-exception contract. errno,
incoming exceptions and the rounding mode are preserved; accuracy assumes
the default IEEE round-to-nearest environment.

The independent MPFR600/mpmath640 corpus covers integer neighbors, table and
half-table grids, tiny/overflow/normal-subnormal limits, subnormal midpoints,
and multiple random input distributions. The larger run is reproducible:

## Real cube root

`gmath_cbrt` uses a seed and Newton correction derived directly from the
binomial series and cube identity. Normalize |x|=m*2^e with 1<=m<2 entirely
in integer bits, including subnormals. Write e=3q+r with 0<=r<3; the normalized
problem is a=m*2^r in [1,8). Its root is in [1,2), so final scaling by 2^q is
exact and always normal (the smallest result is 2^-358). Restore the input sign
after this scaling. No floating operation acts on the original subnormal input.

For one of 64 midpoint values mj=1+(j+1/2)/64, let s=(m-mj)/mj. The
subtraction is exact, |s|<=1/128, and the reciprocal and cube-root midpoint
constants have independently agreed nearest binary64 values from MPFR and
mpmath. The seed is

```
y = cbrt(mj) * (1 + s*(1/3 + s*(-1/9 + s*5/81))) * 2^(r/3).
```

Let u=2^-53. The exact fourth derivative remainder is bounded by
(10/243)*(1-1/128)^(-4)*(1/128)^4, below 1.6e-10 relative. The midpoint subtraction
is exact. Reciprocal rounding and its multiplication perturb s by at most
3*u/128. Bounding each nested polynomial operation and coefficient rounding,
then the root/factor constants and two final products, by 16*u relative is a
conservative allowance. Together these leave the relative seed error
|delta|<2^-32, including all rounded operations.

A compensated square y*y and product (y*y)*y recover the cube as high+low.
The error-free product helpers' partial products neither overflow nor become
subnormal. Taking y<3 conservatively, |square.lo|<=9*u and each cube tail is
bounded by 27*u. The rounded square.lo*y and final tail addition lose at most
81*u^2 in absolute terms. Since a and the high cube are close, a-high is exact
by Sterbenz. Rounding residual=(a-high)-low contributes less than 2^-80
absolute error: |a-y^3|<25*2^-32, so its rounding loss is at most u times that
quantity, in addition to the much smaller tail loss.

Compute correction=residual/((3*y)*y), root=y+correction. The denominator
has relative error below 3*u; the division adds at most u. The exact correction
has magnitude below 2^-30 and the denominator exceeds 2, so the total absolute
error in the computed correction is below 2^-78. In exact arithmetic Newton's
remaining relative error is delta^2*(3+2*delta)/(3*(1+delta)^2), below 2^-63.
Since the true normalized root is below 2, the pre-addition root error is below
2^-61 after allowing both errors. In [1,2), final addition costs at most half
an ulp; the 2^-61 reserve is less than 0.002 ulp. If that pre-rounded value crosses
1 or 2, it remains much nearer that endpoint than half its neighboring spacing
and rounds to the endpoint. Thus these conservative bounds remain below
0.502 ulp. The public target remains one ulp, rather than correct rounding.

All working values are normalized and far from the exponent limits, including
split products and residuals. Signed zeros/infinities return unchanged; NaNs
canonicalize and signaling NaNs raise invalid. Binary powers with e divisible
by three bypass approximation and return exactly with no exceptions. Other
exact cubes may raise inexact. errno, incoming flags and rounding mode remain
unchanged; the numerical bound assumes round-to-nearest.

The reference generator independently rounds MPFR600 and mpmath640 values,
then certifies both the binary64 root and binary32 signed-error word using
exact rational cube comparisons at their neighboring rounding midpoints.
The true root ULP comes independently from floor(input_exponent/3). Ties use
even parity; exact cubes require zero error. Seed/factor roots also have exact
rounding-cell certificates, and the generated header must reproduce byte for
byte. A certificate disagreement fails generation rather than accepting an
uncertain answer.

## Binary32 basic operations

`floatbasic.c` derives floor/ceil/trunc/round/rint from the binary32 encoding.
Magnitudes at least `2^23` are already integers. Below `1`, explicit comparisons
with `0`/`0.5` choose signed zero or signed one. In the remaining range, exponent e
identifies `23-e` fractional bits: removing them truncates exactly; incrementing
the remaining magnitude by its integer unit implements floor/ceil and the
halfway rules. Every possible resulting integer is exactly representable as a
float. No finite floating arithmetic is needed; even subnormal inputs are
handled as integer bits. `rintf` uses ties to even independently of host mode,
as the double API does; `roundf` uses ties away from zero.

`fabsf` clears the sign and `copysignf` copies only y's sign bit. A NaN x
returns the fixed float NaN `0x7fc00000`, raising invalid only for signaling x.
A NaN y, including a signaling one, contributes its sign without arithmetic or
an exception. These seven APIs raise no other exceptions and preserve errno,
incoming flags and mode.

`sqrtf` exactly widens its float input to the existing correctly rounded double
square root and narrows once. This does not suffer double rounding. Normalize
a positive input to a in [1,4), on a grid no finer than `2^-23`, with the root in
[1,2). A binary32 root midpoint z has odd numerator times `2^-24`, so z² has odd
numerator on the `2^-48` grid and cannot equal a. Hence |a-z²|>=2^-48; dividing
by sqrt(a)+z<4 gives |sqrt(a)-z|>2^-50. Double sqrt rounding contributes at
most `2^-53` in the normalized range, safely below that separation, so its result
and the true root lie on the same side of every float midpoint. Scaling is
exact; every positive finite float root is normal, from sqrt(2^-149) upward.
Float NaN inputs are classified before widening or entering the double NaN
path. This prevents the extra invalid observed for quiet float NaNs on SPARC64.
NaN results from negative arguments are explicitly packed into canonical float
bits after the double kernel, so cross-format NaN sign/payload conversion
cannot vary them.

Signed zeros and positive infinity survive; negative inputs give canonical NaN
with invalid. Other NaNs canonicalize, signaling NaNs raise invalid. Positive
finite calls may raise inexact even for exact roots because the portable double
kernel's intermediate arithmetic can be inexact; no overflow, underflow or
divide-by-zero occurs. Numerical accuracy assumes nearest; state preservation is
checked under all modes.

The eight committed corpora use exactly widened input/result words for the
existing hash probe. Fraction integer arithmetic and pinned MPFR rint modes
independently agree for rounding; sqrt results are rounded directly in MPFR24
and certified against exact rational squares of their binary32 rounding-cell
midpoints. Raw signaling float NaNs are unit cases because widening quiets
them. Tests also require exact metadata counts, unique inputs/pairs, valid
minimum-size overrides, raw special bits, each/all incoming flags and errno.
